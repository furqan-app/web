# 0.1 — Light and gold variants in the lab

## Summary

Make the reader lab theme-switchable and re-derive the design language for light and gold. Every atmospheric value in the lab today is a function of a near-black desk; light and gold need the same *rules* expressed in new *values*, worked out where nothing production depends on the answer.

## Approach

Extend the lab's route-scoped `--rl-*` block from one set of values into three, selected by the existing theme class on `<html>` (`.theme-light .fq-reader-lab`, `.theme-gold .fq-reader-lab`, `.theme-dark .fq-reader-lab`). Add a lab-local theme switcher so all three can be compared without leaving the route — it may write the real theme preference, because this is the one lab control that is allowed to be live; note it explicitly in the sheet as such.

The hard problems, in order:

1. **The lamp.** On dark it is a warm pool at 7.5% over near-black. On a light desk a brightening pool is invisible — the light case likely inverts to a *shading* gradient (a soft darkening toward the edges) with the lamp expressed as an absence of shade. Derive, do not guess.
2. **The vignette.** Same inversion problem, same treatment.
3. **Depth.** Dark carries lift with a lighter face and a warm rim because a shadow measures ~1 point on `(7,15,23)`. Light and gold have shadow headroom, so the *rule* ("the folio sits on a surface") stays and the *values* change to a real cast. This is the ADR 0032 model: shared rule, per-theme values.
4. **The two accents.** Gold-on-dark is legible at `39 56% 57%`. On the gold theme's parchment background, gold-as-identity may collapse into the surface — that is the single most likely failure of the whole language, and this subtask exists partly to find out.

## Decision Tree / Algorithm

| Condition | Value strategy |
|---|---|
| Theme background is dark (`.theme-dark`) | Keep the derived lab values as-is; they are the reference. |
| Theme background is light or parchment | Re-derive: brightening atmospheric layers become shading layers; rim-light lift becomes cast-shadow lift. |
| An accent no longer separates from its background | Retune that theme's accent token — never introduce a third accent, and never scope the rule by theme. |
| A value is chosen | Verify by sampling rendered pixels, not by reading the declaration. |

## Findings

1. **The lamp's carrier channel is a property of the medium.** Dark and gold express it in lightness, light in temperature (+28 R−B centre vs edge, ~0 L). Recorded in [ADR 0047 Addendum](../../../architecture/adr/0047-adopt-reader-lab-design-language.md).
2. **The pool's extent is a per-theme value too.** The first light and gold lamps reused dark's tight `58% 62%` and measured **exactly zero**: the pool centres under the folio, so on a wide desk its visible pixels all fall past the falloff. Light and gold need a wide, weak pool (`98% 92%` / `94% 88%`).
3. **Gold-as-identity survives parchment — as bronze, not metal.** The predicted failure was real for *bright* gold; the fix is the value, not the rule.
4. **The gold theme's live accent must not be its own `--primary`** (warm gold), or identity and live state become one colour. Emerald is the live accent in all three themes.
5. **The identity medallion keeps a dark face in every theme** — a struck seal is a material, and inverting it would leave the white logo mark invisible.
6. Two lint errors (`UserMenu.tsx` unused import, `use-close-on-back-gesture.ts` two `any`s) are inherited from `main` at `1283e3a`; both files belong to subtask 3.2, which fixes them.

## Files to Change

- `app/globals.css` — lab token block becomes three theme-scoped value sets under `.fq-reader-lab` / `.fq-reader-lab-settings`. Still route-scoped; nothing enters `:root`. Every atmospheric, depth, chrome, well, scrim and medallion literal that was hard-coded in a rule is now a token, so the two new blocks declare values only.
- `app/components/reader-lab/use-lab-theme.ts` (new) — route-local theme switching that moves the `<html>` class without writing the preference, and restores the entry theme on unmount.
- `app/components/reader-lab/ReaderLabSettingsSidebar.tsx` — appearance section gains a live three-way theme switcher, labelled as the one live control in the panel; sheet surface and scrim move from arbitrary values to tokenised classes.
- `messages/ar.json`, `messages/en.json` — keys for the theme switcher and its "this control is live" note; `readerLab.appearanceDarkOnly` retired.

## Constraints

- Route-scoped only. No `--rl-*` token may be declared on `:root`, `.dark`, or an existing theme block in this subtask.
- Depth rules stay shared across themes; only values differ (ADR 0032).
- Verify every atmospheric and depth value by sampling rendered pixels in all three themes.
- The lab remains unlinked; no navigation entry point is added.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not derive light and gold by algorithmically inverting dark's values — the media are not symmetric.
- Do not add a drop shadow to the dark variant to "match" light.
- Do not introduce a third accent to rescue gold-on-parchment.
- Do not begin production token work here; that is subtask 2.1.

## Decisions Made

- The lab's theme switcher is the single exception to the lab's inert-controls rule, because comparing themes is the entire point of this subtask.
- Gold-on-parchment legibility is an open question this subtask is expected to answer, including with a negative result. **Answered: it survives, as bronze.**
- **The switcher moves the theme class but does not persist it.** This plan permitted writing the real preference; `INDEX.md`'s What-NOT-to-Do forbids a Phase 0 subtask writing user preferences. Not writing satisfies both, and it costs nothing — the comparison needs the class, not the storage key. The entry theme is restored when the route unmounts.
- The identity medallion is theme-invariant (dark face, warm rim). It is the one surface in the language that does not follow its theme.
- Depth on light and gold is a real cast (~9 L under the folio), and the rim becomes a white highlight rather than a warm catch-light. Same rule, different amplitude — ADR 0032's model, applied outward.
